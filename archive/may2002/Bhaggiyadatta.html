Articles · May 2002

Give ’em Enough Rope

Krisantha Sri Bhaggiyadatta


I have realized that the more appropriate Sinhala translation of this
firstcoming journal LINES is not "IRI" or the more sanskritized "REKHA,"
but the much more connotative "LANU" which would be a fine name for a
Singlish journal, with the word's cerebral sparks of "giving someone a line
or the rope," and Lenin's sanguine prospect that we'll be able to sell the
beast some lines to hang themself with.

I couldn't decide which poems to send you from a sixthcoming collection of
missives. The poems I have written about living six point five degrees up
from the equator, should be published more appropriately here first rather
than virtually on that Euro-settler-controlled highway. Though where here I
don't know, cos most Angloid publications let alone thought processes are
part of that same colonized (now goebbelized) road - they say that English
here, like the Kandy Road, needs the Brutish Council, and is the only way
to go for Anguished poetry in these parts. So I don't want to even call
there anymore, even though it is hard to avoid English or white[ned like
myself] people in this country especially if one locates oneself strictly
to the wannabes, the hasbeens, the beenthere-donethat native informants who
are always on the lookout for a new thrill, bit of colour or tidbit
research to supply their Euro-Yankee paymasters, and whose main form of
literateuri now is actually 'proposal writing.' Yes, who does one primarily
and actually write for when one publishes in any language, let alone now
the US Treasury's English?

"It's a source of irony
for all de world 2 see
Dey talkin human
rights and livin
in Washington DC"
(-to take liberties with Gil Scott Heron)


In November 1999, I found myself with my bags, change of clothing slung
over my right ulna, assorted A4s of my poems in my left fist, my black ass
crushed among hundreds of people (including the wannabes, the hasbeens, the
beenthere-donethat folks) at the Nuyorican Poet's Cafe. Here I was, almost
end-of-the-year, end-of-the-century, near the very loud backside of the
beast, waiting to read, heart pumping in the NUYO's very hyper Friday Night
Poetry Slam.

There had been some screwup, and having come all the way from Toronto by
10-hour train the night before (and from Beijing a month before that and
from Colombo three months before that), I found myself, without knowing
exactly who was in the audience, suddenly off the 42nd-street train
platform and thrust unceremoniously on stage, by my otherwise wonderful
hosts, the Nuyorican poet Pedro Pietri and the stocky, dreadlocked, dark
longcoated members of the LIMCWM - Latin Insomniacs Motorcycle Club
Without Motorcycles, and the organizers of the Nuyorican Café,.

Pedro was one of the founders of the Cafe along with Amiri Baraka and other
poets who wanted to build a liberating artspace for African and
Puerto-Rican prolelaureates. (Nuyorican being their own name for the
vibrant NY Puerto Rican community, which has more Ricans than PR's capital,
San Jose!, and whose people seem, like Sri Lankans, to register a swathe of
vast international history)


don't talk about it
don't shout about it
don't complain
don't whine
just get up there
and do it
[Advice before I read at the Nuyorican]


I read forgettably the first night before the opening of Pedro Pietri's
eerily-ordinary surreal play, El Living Room, and then depressed, find
myself reception to some of the zaniness of the islet-town's mentalscape.
One of the actresses, half-jokingly I hoped, autographed my programme
saying, "Dahlink, this zignature is gonna be worth lotza money in a few
yahz, you wait and zee." She then left nose-leading stage right and I
realized she was seriuz!!! It's been a few yahz now so I assume she was
pumping off the hype one must perspirate amidst the sparkling slimelights
of Manhattan, the very centre of 21st century capital's media
miscommunication monopolies, this side of the NYSE that side of Wall
Street: hoping one of their organic agents may be there ready to pick her
up in this off-off-off-off Broadway venue in primetime for a career reading
their goodnight valium-atives on CNN, Fox or BBC.

"Stars they come and go
They come fast, they go slow
They go like the last light
Of the sun
All in a blaze
And all you see is glory"
[Nina Simone's words I think]

Artists, like Teachers, think themselves a caste apart. They may look down
on policemen or soldiers, and may think they and their thoughts are bereft
of the seamier process-seas of life. They may claim they are property to
the higher consciousnesses in living. But now it was Friday Night. The
weekly opening to the Holy-Days. Everyone hopefully have their johnny
paycheques cashed and their paler desires multiplied. There's a long
lineup outside this dilapidated yet grand tabernacle to poetry by the
wintry north atlantic, featuring a squeeze of bright-eyed, fashion-edged
young people. Mainly young white suburban UColumbia-going types doing the
slum, several of them matty-blonde-haired simulating African dreadlocks.
There was the sensitive white girl reading incest; the shiny muscular black
intellectual rappin' the void. And me, I was nerve-bundled and ready to
kill. I had been knocked off top-billing by a very slick but empty
mid-western rap-ist, full of the sashayed-moves that MTV markets, and all I
could think of was, I was gonna show them what poetry - no, I, my dark self
- is all about. I? Them?

Quite a few seemed to be veterans of these insular artistic wars, and knew
each other, but not with the wry self-mockery of out-of-work musicians at
the Food Stamps counter. They were still prize pugilists at a
press-confereed weigh-in in Las Vegas, despite the air of democracy: anyone
could participate, and be slotted into teams for the Slam and divvied up,
with judges chosen from different sections of the crowd. I felt within
myself all the venomous juices of being, less a worker competing in the big
bad labour market, even less a worker communing with others, but a small
trader in a large supermarket with heavy overheads having to come up with a
new shill to snare up a customer. And I was beginning to feel sick.

At the same time, I couldn't back off. I was in the ring. Poetry was now a
contest. The bars had decided it was cheaper than musicians, cheaper than a
jukebox, cheaper than cathode-ray karaoke, to have a few egotists and
traumatics come and rant or mumble into a lonely mike all for the price of
a cover charged entry into their watery-beer-rooms to be slammed by an
audience. And thus US poetry was being shaped for the new century. Much
like how earlier the newspaper column came up with the shorter lines of
modern poetry cos one was paid by the pica line. Though the NUYO is very
different, and the atmos was somewhat lighthearted, laidback, at the same
time, I knew I had to "show 'em." Then again, "damn," I thought, "how the
hey did I get here? Where did I get these killer feelins' from? Had Giu's
(Chinese for 'the devil's) York got to me? Or brought out these clearly
dormant yearnings if not instincts, to compete and slam backS

Almost 20 years before, a friend returning from New York had brought me a
poster from this very café in its more crimson days. The poster had a
picture of a caricatured masked white man, pointing a finger much like the
"I WANT YOU" WW2 posters featuring a tall-hatted Uncle Sam calling on
pimpled recruits (for another penetrative campaign in Asia). But this
poster said instead: "WHAT IS THE RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN THE LONE RANGER AND THE MEANS OF PRODUCTION?" It was announcing a reading by Amiri Baraka and other Black and Puerto Rican poets. I loved it. (It would be many years before I read the Soviet writer Bakhtin's critique of the mechanical reduction of comparing ideological products to the instruments of
production alone).

I thought the NUYO poster was terrific and had it on my living-room wall. I
still cherish it. That there was a space to perform such poetry that spoke
to the struggles of the moments was cool. Masked white men run these
countries. Police Budgets across North America were taking on Vietnamic
proportions and there had to be non-capital crime to fit the budget, so the
demonization of black people, particularly youth, was ready raised for full
baton-swing, north and south. We were trying to create such an art space in
Toronto that would fuel the resistance against a host of odds, where poetry
was controlled by the varsity-centric Canadian-subsidy types full of white
sound, and happy and well-rewarded, to signify nothing. But here was poetry
related to the refractions of reality and the reflections of pseudoreality.

I was also ecstatic to be given a copy of Pedro Pietri's classic book of
poems, Puerto Rican Obituary which had been just published by the steadfast
NY Marxist journal, The Monthly Review. I had taken seriously back then
some satirical PR floating around that the poet had OD-ed and was no longer
with the living. So I had spent those 20 years popularizing this
supposedly dead-poet's dead-on verses in Toronto, shouting it out, along
with my own, wherever and whenever I could. I remember reading his poem
early one dawn on the picket-line for an Ontario-wide labour strike: "woke
up this morning / feeling excellent / picked up the phone / dialed the
number of / my Equal Opportunity employer / to inform him / I will not be
in work today / "Are you sick?" the boss asked me / "No Sir" I replied / I
am feeling too good / to report to work today / if I feel sick tomorrow / I
will come in early!")

So 20 years later I get my chance to read my own poetry in that cafe and
meet the very-much-alive poet himself in New York. Tanisha, my niece (who
Pedro is the only one to outdo at the rate of rapid understatement), had
called to inform me in Beijing that she'd met Pedro in Toronto, and he
wanted me to come read in NY, November! Wow! (I was far from Toronto when
Pedro finally performed there, but a local radio station also called me to
transoceanically introduce this Manhattanic minstrel!!!)

Sri Lankans and Puerto Ricans I was soon to discover had too much in
common, more than just being islandic and too-long-colonized. Pedro's
studied understatement in this case also meant that we ended up at the
wrong station or exit on 42nd Street but eventually found ourselves being
hugged by Pedro and Papoleto, his sidekick from the Latin Insomniacs
Motorcycle Club Without Motorcycles. Our hosts then shanghaied us to the
38th floor of the Artists' Co-op, right across from the silly ball they
light up 31st nights at Times Square, where I find out that Pedro has set
up a Puerto Rican embassy in his one bedroom apartment declaring Puerto
Rico independent. There is no furniture. He even hands me a passport. He
stamps it.

We stare out over the neon void of the New York night "See where the lights
end that's Brooklyn n' that's Harlem 'n see those lights thems the tallest
buildings in the world" I mutter, "I wish I was Joshua, and I wish I had
a trumpet," referring to the prophet who brought down the walls of Jericho
with his horn. "Keep dreamin'," someone sez, "it'll never happen" "You're
a damn anarchist!" says Pedro.

So there I was then (9.11.99), crushed black ass, going out for red-eye
smokes every few minutes, in between the Slam and after the 'main act' had
come and gone, and me waiting to poeticize under capital's towering
shadowsS My name is called out. I am now blinded by the lights. I had in
my hand my latest book, my "Wha' Kinda Indian Arr U?" (epic) poem, and I
finally decided that all this pugilistic posturing was to be shoved aside
with a simple shrug. And act as if that white g[l]aze was not there: the
audience looking so pink and smug, as if they were the epitome and arbiters
of bad poetry, and waiting for this coolie to get it over with so they
could get down to the order of the day.

I started off by saying: "When I came to North America they told me I was
an Indian. I was then told that the real people of this continent are also
called Indian." "So tell me," I said, trying to look like Columbus hand
above the eyes sneerin' into the interior of the cafe: "Wha Kinda Indian
Arr You?" Then I leaned into full press: "Just for the record, I was born
in Sri Lanka." Clinton had just ordered bombings of Iraq from the
nuclearized island of Diego Garcia, just south of Sri Lanka. "Sri Lanka is
just north of Diego Garcia..."

1800 seconds later, there is not a sound, and then I see Tanisha and Pedro
fisting the air in the background, and all the black people in the audience
noddin, "tha's right!!!" I feel thrilled. I'm lazy, I have to always
rely on an audience to gear me, to really fly. It was Black people's quiet
murmurs, the occasional snorts and laughter, and it is this subsonic call
and response that leads me around those blanker eyes. A Chinese suit and
tie comes over and firmly shakes my hand. I see some other Asians ducking
for cover. Several other Black poets come exchange their CDs and books with
mine.

The next day, I slip right into the groove with the Latin jazz band I had
never even got to rehearse with. It zings like a dream. It is only later I
wonder about the demons and emotions and juices I had called upon to deal
with my foolish anxieties about 'performing.' I am just happy to feel I
have demoralized a whole set o' white folk and up-pressed a whole set o'
other folk... A-men!!! But do I really like this 'performing monkey'
business? Isn't the real art: changing the world? What happens when: this
poetic piggy goes to marketS

Later that night, a television host asks me: if I had come to new york for
validation, as before artists went to rome, london, paris... I feel real
offended. Well, I shouldn'ta been sooo so. I shoulda been cool and said,
Va-li-daa-tion? By this? By you? I steups instead: "Chaaa you crazy? I
came to meet Pedro Pietri and read at the Nuyorican Poets' Cafe... I'd been
told he was dead..." Pedro replies: "I still am and livin' in New York."

Aay Wha' Kinda Indian Arr U?

am I a near Indian or a far Indian or a very very much

lost Indian or 'jes Indian,

an eas'Indian or a wes'Indian eas'Indian or

the central Indian in a cast of outcastes

in a chapaati-western movie...

a western oriental, an accidental occidental

or the disoriental

getting up on the right side of the bed

every unemployed afternoon...

the 100%noble aboriginal dravidian - the original druid

dreaming darkly of Ravana, of Harappa,

of Taxila, of MohendaroŠ

the original brown aryan

awaiting honorary white membership

in de white aryan brotherhood...or

am I an achchaaru a massaala a sambola sambaal

Š.a diasporic, a disparate, or a just plain desh-perateŠ

am I ali baba, sai baba or the homeless baba

going baa baa black sheep have you any

po-co-lo-co-po-mo post-colonial Indians

pursuing mid-colonial postings at columbia and yale?Š

an un-tenured intellectual coolie, a denizen

of that gated that green-carded silly-clone valley

who invents a better design on the lock

to his own personal gas chamber...

a manual coolie who keeps the cool

under a thick blue collar

(Kuli means Wages in Tamil & Sinhala...

Kuli in Chinese means Bitter Strength...

am I the original Indian heroine

in the original mucho music video

slinking hips around a plastic coconut tree

in a mississippi masala motel

owned by one patel -yaaah!!!Š

am I the tenth uncounted Indian

in the ten little Indians,

or am I the one and lonely in a nation of one billion...

namaaste! salai malai kum!

sasree kal! aubowan! vanakam!

was I the Indian that Columbus indicated

he'd wanted to meet

the Indian these still lost children of Columbus

have yet to discover...

Was I the Yankee-go-patriot disguised as an Indian

at the Boston Tea Party, or was I the only real Indian,

or was I the thrown-over tea

and was the american revolution really

against the Indian in me?Š

am I the Indian judge who allowed

Union Carbide's CEO Warren Anderson to leave

behind the tattered lungs of 10,000 people in Bhopal...

(wha' kinda terrorists arr those, these CEOs?)

am I Kalpana Dutt who raided Chittagong armoury in 1930

Kalpana Dutt - "seen about wearing men's clothes..."

am I Bhagat Singh who saw the link

between capitalism and imperialism,

between communism and freedom

am I Preeti Wadedar, she, who died leading the attack

on the Railway Officer's Club in 1932...

or, am I the original pacific Indian

Mahatma Gandhi, waiting for a British pass

to preach non-violence to the soon-to-be-slaughtered

or, the pencil sharpener who was given a day off

while Mountbatten scratched those lines

into the brown-paper face of my motherŠ

am I Tonto or

the Lone tee vee ranger and frequent channel changer,

asking kemoy sabay wasichu gringo honky

gweilo sahib vellai sudho: "wha' we doin' baws?"

"wha' we gonna see nex'?"Š

the Indian who says Marx and Engels

stole their theories from the Vedas & the Koran...

the Indian who says Marxism is a western concept

but plays the capital markets from New York, &

sells the national heirlooms, the sweat'n'blood banks

from unnumbered accounts in davos, switzerland...

or am I the neo-conservative neo-colonial neo-coconut neo-con Indian who

thinks Thatcher is one of us

because her father owned a corner shop...

am I the tantric who has sex without coming,

and doesn't even show up for the act...the she/he/it

who knows all the positions of the Kama Sutra

forwards, backwards and auto-manually...

am I the Indian listening to a sermon

on why homosexuality is

unIndian, from a holy man of the cloth

who has yogurt stains on the hem of his long robeŠ

the Indian who wants to replace Mother Teresa (MT) Inc.

be her After Life Inc. as well

fly around the world showing dictators: errors

in their charitable accounting methods...

am I the Indian guru born-again rajneesh

providing hilary clinton with an inner healthcare policy,

running transcend-mental (TM) peacekeeping missions

between the oval office and the kennedy bedroom...

am I the next Indian prophet of non-violence

to senator jesse kkk helms

& them tax-sheltered hare krishnas of c.i.a. virginia..

am I the Indian wearing salwar or a sari or a pottu

on the subway platform at 10pm...

am I the sponsored Indian whose husband owns her

for ten years or else thanks again, to the immigration department's

department of familiar values...

am I the Indian kept 5 oceans apart from her lover?

am I the Indian hiding in a women's shelter

from her "till death do us apart" husbandŠ

am I the Attorney General of BC

- said to be south asian (applause, applause) declaring mega-war on the

Shuswap Sundancers at Gustafsen Lake

- he claims a distant uncle was hanged by the British

fighting for India's independence, but which

distant Indian is he, & which distant Indian is they and

which distant Indian am i?

& who should be hung now?
